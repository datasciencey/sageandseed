Research and practice

Evidence, guidance, and development—clearly separated

We summarize what sources support and what remains uncertain. Sage & Seed does not claim that social conversation treats or cures dementia.

Published research

The Effect of Information Communication Technology Interventions on Reducing Social Isolation in the Elderly: A Systematic Review

Chen, Y. R. R., & Schulz, P. J. · Journal of Medical Internet Research, 18(1), e18 · 2016

This systematic review found emerging evidence that information and communication technologies can support social contact, connectedness, and some dimensions of loneliness among older adults. Results differed by technology, population, and outcome.

What the evidence supports: ICT can help some older adults maintain or expand social connection. What remains uncertain: its effect on social isolation as a whole, who benefits most, and how benefits persist over time.

Effectiveness of Technology Interventions in Addressing Social Isolation, Connectedness, and Loneliness in Older Adults: Systematic Umbrella Review

Balki, E., Hayes, N., & Holland, C. · JMIR Aging, 5(4), e40125 · 2022

This umbrella review synthesized 21 moderate-quality reviews covering varied technology interventions. It identified promising approaches alongside substantial differences in study populations, technologies, measures, and methods.

What the evidence supports: well-designed technology can create routes to connection. What remains uncertain: generalizable effectiveness and which intervention features matter most.

Social Isolation and Loneliness in Older Adults: Opportunities for the Health Care System

National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine · National Academies Press · 2020

This consensus report distinguishes objective social isolation from the subjective experience of loneliness and examines their health implications, assessment, education, and the role of health care and community partnerships.

What the evidence supports: social isolation and loneliness deserve systematic attention and cross-sector coordination. What remains uncertain: the most effective interventions for different people and settings.

Video Calls for Reducing Social Isolation and Loneliness in Older People

Noone, C., McSharry, J., Smalle, M., et al. · Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews · 2020

This rapid review included three small studies involving 201 participants in nursing homes. The available evidence did not establish that video calls reduce loneliness, and certainty was limited.

What the evidence supports: video calls can be studied as a means of social contact. What remains uncertain: their effects on loneliness, depression, and quality of life across settings.
