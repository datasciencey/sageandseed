# Sage & Seed Content Guide

## Core promise

Every resource should help one young person engage one older adult more comfortably, personally, and meaningfully.

## Voice

- Warm, calm, plainspoken, and adult-to-adult.
- Describe connection as reciprocal.
- Prefer “older adult” or “person living with dementia” to labels such as “the elderly” or “demented person.”
- Use “young conversation partner” or “young participant,” not caregiver, unless that is the person’s actual role.
- Talk about abilities, interests, preferences, and relationships—not deficits alone.

## Activity writing pattern

Each guide includes purpose, materials, preparation, opening language, sample prompts, personalization, signs of engagement, simplification, redirection, and a follow-up. Treat activities as invitations. Never reward speed, accuracy, completion, streaks, or competition.

## Evidence and claims

- Separate published research, practice guidance, and Sage & Seed development.
- Link the original source and state uncertainty.
- Do not claim treatment, prevention, cure, or guaranteed changes in mood, cognition, loneliness, or quality of life.
- Never invent testimonials, participant outcomes, partnerships, or scale.

## Privacy and safeguarding

- Use anonymized examples.
- Do not publish personal or medical information.
- Confirm consent and authorization for photos, stories, recordings, and AI use.
- Send safety concerns through the approved safeguarding process; do not investigate or promise secrecy.

## Accessibility checklist

- Use descriptive headings and links.
- Write meaningful alt text; leave decorative images with empty alt text.
- Keep paragraphs short and instructions concrete.
- Preserve keyboard navigation, visible focus, contrast, large targets, and print usability.
- Avoid auto-playing media and motion that cannot be reduced.
