# Translation Review Notes

## Review completed

Hindi and Kannada resources were checked for preservation of: person-first framing, adult-to-adult respect, voluntary choice, non-testing language, emotional validation, safeguarding, escalation, and uncertainty of evidence. Clinical claims were not strengthened in translation. Familiar borrowed terms are now written first in the local script with the English spelling in parentheses—for example, `म्यूज़िक (music)`, `डॉक्टर (doctor)`, `ಮ್ಯೂಸಿಕ್ (music)`, and `ಡಾಕ್ಟರ್ (doctor)`. Roman-script English is not used as the primary form inside localized guidance.

## Localization choices

- Hindi examples use conversational phrasing, cricket, familiar family language, and names such as Meera/Riya without implying one religion or region.
- Kannada examples use Bengaluru-compatible contemporary mixed usage while avoiding the assumption that every Kannada speaker shares the same food, faith, cinema, or festival preferences.
- Both languages present examples as possibilities and direct the reader back to the individual’s own culture, language, neighborhood, work, and interests.

## Native-speaker review recommended before public launch

1. Review mixed English/Hindi and English/Kannada terms with the actual audience (high-school students and frontline caregivers) to calibrate how much English feels natural.
2. Review forms of address and gendered Hindi constructions for the intended regions and organizations.
3. Review Kannada register across urban and rural audiences; several operational terms intentionally remain in English.
4. Confirm preferred local terminology for acute change, escalation, safeguarding, and supervisory channels with each participating care organization.
5. Conduct an accessibility read-aloud test for every translated card and starter guide.

These localizations are publication-ready drafts, not certified translations. Native-speaker and safeguarding review remains appropriate before operational use.
